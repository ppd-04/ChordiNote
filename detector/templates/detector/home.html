How It Works

Four simple steps from audio to chords

1

Upload Your Audio

Drop any .wav or .mp3 file — a song, a guitar recording, or even your voice humming a melody.

2

Signal Decomposition

The audio is split into tiny windows. Each window is analyzed using FFT to extract frequency peaks.

3

Note & Chroma Mapping

Frequencies are mapped to the 12 musical pitch classes (C, C#, D... B) creating a chroma profile.

4

Chord Matching

The chroma profile is compared against known chord templates to identify the most likely chord.

Two Detection Methods

Compare time-domain vs frequency-domain approaches

Autocorrelation

Time-Domain

  • Slides the signal over itself to find repeating patterns
  • The lag with highest correlation = the wave period
  • Frequency = 1 / Period
  • Best for clean, single-note signals

FFT Spectrum

Frequency-Domain

  • Transforms audio into a frequency amplitude spectrum
  • Reveals all simultaneous frequencies at once
  • Chroma features group harmonics into note classes
  • Better for complex chords and polyphonic audio
